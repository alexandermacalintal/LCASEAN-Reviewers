# LCASEAN — Connections and comparisons

**Working edition:** Based on modules 01–04 and the three fully text-read sources in working module 05. Connections to unread economic, social and maritime readings are questions for further study, not completed source syntheses. See COVERAGE_REPORT.md.

## 1. State-building → sovereignty → ASEAN's institutional design

New states needed territorial authority, domestic stability and independence from outside domination. These conditions help explain governments' attachment to noninterference and sovereign equality. ASEAN's intergovernmental design enables cooperation without demanding political merger.

**Trade-off:** sovereignty safeguards can make membership acceptable while constraining intrusive regional remedies. The Charter nevertheless includes implementation obligations, human-rights commitments and dispute procedures; sovereignty is not the same as absence of obligations.

[Weatherbee, pp. 7–9, 13–22; Charter, pp. 7–13, 26–29; modules 01–04.]

## 2. Earlier rivalry → repeated consultation → trust, but not automatic unity

Konfrontasi and Sabah demonstrate why cooperation among the founding states cannot be assumed to have been natural. Almonte sees practical cooperation as a way to build political strength and common purpose. Acharya shows why this achievement does not guarantee a common position on every later issue.

**Distinction:** a history of cooperation is an asset, not an irreversible elimination of national interests.

[S20, pp. 2–3; S18, pp. 5–8; module 02.]

## 3. ASEAN Way → legal institutions: coexistence rather than simple replacement

Goh emphasizes private consultation and nonconfrontation. The Charter defines organs, obligations and settlement procedures while preserving consensus. ASEAN can therefore become more rules-based without becoming supranational or abandoning consultation.

**Compare:**
- Procedure: how governments communicate.
- Principle: what they promise to respect.
- Institution: who acts and under what mandate.
- Outcome: whether a problem is actually addressed.

[S26, pp. 1–3; Charter, pp. 12–29; modules 04–05.]

## 4. Enlargement → representation and capacity pressures

A geographically broader association is more regionally representative, but differences in national preferences, political systems and development levels complicate common action. Concord II calls for narrowing development gaps; Acharya links wider tasks and membership to coordination burdens.

**Do not infer:** that newer members alone explain every failure. Domestic/regime interests also affect founding members.

[Concord II, pp. 2, 5, 11; S18, pp. 5–6; modules 03, 05.]

## 5. Consensus → acceptance and potential blockage

Consensus protects members from simple numerical domination and may strengthen acceptance. It also makes coordinated action difficult when national preferences diverge. Economic Minus X is an agreed implementation flexibility—not a general abolition of consensus.

**External link:** a divided ASEAN has less capacity to give substantive direction in regional forums, even when those forums remain ASEAN-led.

[Charter, pp. 26–27; S18, pp. 5–8; modules 04–05.]

## 6. Three pillars → different functions within shared problems

| Problem | Political-security aspect | Economic aspect | Socio-cultural aspect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cross-border production | Stable relations and reliable routes | Customs, investment and market access | Skills, working conditions and inclusion |
| Displacement | Conflict, border management and diplomacy | Livelihood disruption | Human welfare, health and protection |
| Regional emergency | Political coordination | Transport/supply disruptions | Public health and disaster response |

These are teaching applications of the pillar framework, not claims that particular historical operations followed this exact sequence.

[Concord II, pp. 3–7; module 04's hypothetical emergency; S20, p. 3.]

## 7. Cambodia → tension between sovereignty and protection

Vietnam's intervention, Khmer Rouge atrocities, representation at the UN and later peace-building cannot be reduced to one moral or legal slogan. Goh records active ASEAN diplomacy while also describing retention of Democratic Kampuchea's representation. Evaluate who benefited, what principle was defended, what instruments were used and which ethical costs remained.

**Exam method:** distinguish conflict outbreak, international diplomatic struggle, peace settlement and later accession. Cambodia was not an ASEAN member at the initial outbreak.

[S26, pp. 3–6; module 02; module 05.]

## 8. Major-power competition → centrality as a conditional role

ASEAN-led forums can be acceptable because stronger powers distrust one another. This creates a platform role without making ASEAN materially dominant. Centrality depends partly on external acceptance and partly on internal unity, capacity and credibility.

**Form:** occupying the institutional center. **Substance:** agenda-setting, direction and resolving disagreements. Evidence of form does not establish substance.

[S18, pp. 2–8; Weatherbee, pp. 1–4; modules 01, 05.]

## 9. Official documents versus scholarly/advocacy readings

| Source type | Strongest use | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| Charter/declaration | Stated commitments and legal/institutional rules | Does not alone demonstrate implementation |
| Vision/blueprint | Goals, policy categories and planned actions | Targets are not achieved outcomes |
| Lecture deck | Instructor-supplied concepts and emphasis | Verify typos; attribute the author's viewpoint |
| Scholarship | Explanation, criticism and interpretation | Arguments can disagree and have dated assumptions |
| Historical chapter | Context and chronological development | Do not silently update an older narrative |

## 10. Four reusable essay questions

1. How can stronger institutions coexist with retained sovereignty?
2. When does diplomatic restraint enable cooperation, and when does it impede protection or implementation?
3. Why might a materially weaker organization convene great powers without controlling them?
4. What evidence separates an aspiration, an institutional arrangement and a demonstrated outcome?
