## Findings incorporated into module 05

### S26 — Goh
- Pp. 1–3: distinction between procedural norms and nonintervention; consultation, private diplomacy, nonconfrontation; historical/cultural explanation attributed, not treated as regional essence.
- Pp. 3–6: Cambodia versus OAS/Haiti comparison; different membership contexts, objectives and instruments limit inference.
- Pp. 4–5: ASEAN actively used diplomatic pressure and UN representation politics; Khmer Rouge representation complicates an unqualified success narrative.
- P. 6: positive assessment of nonmilitary ASEAN conflict diplomacy; admission/election-related pressure demonstrates activity rather than pure passivity.
- **Error control:** p. 1 says ASEAN established in 1961. Correct to 1967 using the already-read Bangkok Declaration. The printed page labels are 113–118; reviewer uses physical pp. 1–6.
- Precise day claimed for Cambodia's 1997 crisis is not repeated without corroboration. Broad sequence only.

### S20 — Almonte
- Pp. 1–2: spillovers, great-power rivalry, economic interdependence and inequality as drivers toward cooperation.
- P. 2: warns that calling interstate war virtually unthinkable would overstate reality; political will depends on solidarity/trust.
- Pp. 2, 4: peacekeeping center/force and regional parliament are proposals, not achieved institutions.
- Pp. 1–2: 2004 economic forecasts must not be silently treated as verified 2015 outcomes.
- P. 3: practical cooperation and common purpose; border security can harm livelihoods.
- P. 4: three-pillar proposals; full speech collection not read, only supplied excerpt.

### S18 — Acharya
- Pp. 2–3: several meanings of centrality; wider architecture, not simply ASEAN's internal organization.
- Pp. 3–5: external powers helped constitute centrality; inclusive ASEAN-led forums met their interests amid mutual distrust.
- Pp. 5–7: four challenges—cohesion, neutrality, China's parallel regionalism, pressures on liberal international order.
- P. 7: form versus substance; centrality must be earned, not proclaimed.
- P. 8: skepticism about centrality is compatible with valuing ASEAN; survival/relevance not identical to wider regional leadership.
- Present-day implications are not claimed; analysis is explicitly dated 2017.
